Constitutional amendments timeline

Fifteen major amendments to the Constitution of India and what each one changed.

← Back to home

AmendmentYearKey change
1st Amendment1951Added the Ninth Schedule and Articles 31A and 31B, added Article 15(4), and widened the restrictions on free speech in Article 19(2).
7th Amendment1956Reorganised States largely on linguistic lines and did away with the Part A, B, C and D categories of States.
24th Amendment1971Affirmed Parliament’s power to amend Fundamental Rights. Kesavananda Bharati later placed the basic structure limit on this power.
25th Amendment1971Limited the right to property compensation and added Article 31C.
42nd Amendment1976Wide-ranging changes. Added “socialist”, “secular” and “integrity” to the Preamble and added Fundamental Duties (Part IVA). Parts were struck down in Minerva Mills.
44th Amendment1978Removed the right to property from Fundamental Rights (now Article 300A) and added safeguards on the Emergency, including that Articles 20 and 21 cannot be suspended.
52nd Amendment1985Added the Tenth Schedule, the anti-defection law.
61st Amendment1988Lowered the voting age from 21 to 18 years.
73rd Amendment1992Gave constitutional status to Panchayati Raj institutions (Part IX).
74th Amendment1992Gave constitutional status to urban local bodies (Part IXA).
86th Amendment2002Inserted Article 21A, the right to education for children aged six to fourteen.
99th Amendment2014Created the National Judicial Appointments Commission. The Supreme Court struck it down in 2015 (reported at (2016) 5 SCC 1).
101st Amendment2016Introduced the Goods and Services Tax.
103rd Amendment2019Provided reservation for economically weaker sections (Articles 15(6) and 16(6)).
106th Amendment2023Women’s reservation: one-third of seats for women in the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies, to take effect after the relevant census and delimitation.

No amendments match that search.
