Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023

A chapter-by-chapter student guide to the BNSS, with key sections explained in plain language.

← Back to home
About this guide. This guide follows the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (Act No. 46 of 2023) as published in the Gazette of India. It replaces the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 for proceedings under the new law. Section headings are taken from the official text, and the explanations are our own summaries. They leave out sub-sections, provisos and exceptions. Read the section itself on India Code before you cite it. Last updated: October 2026.

Chapter V: Arrest of PersonsSections 35 to 62＋

Arrest: when police can arrest with or without a warrant, how an arrest is made, the rights of an arrested person and the 24-hour rule.

Key sections explained

  • BNSS 35. When police may arrest without warrant.
    Lists when the police may arrest without a warrant. For some cases the police can instead issue a notice asking the person to appear.Earlier law: CrPC 41, 41A
  • BNSS 43. Arrest how made.
    How an arrest is made.Earlier law: CrPC 46
  • BNSS 47. Person arrested to be informed of grounds of arrest and of right to bail.
    A person arrested must be told the grounds of arrest and, where the offence is bailable, of the right to be released on bail.Earlier law: CrPC 50
  • BNSS 48. Obligation of person making arrest to inform about arrest, etc., to relative or friend.
    The person making the arrest must tell a relative or friend of the arrested person about the arrest.Earlier law: CrPC 50A
  • BNSS 58. Person arrested not to be detained more than twenty-four hours.
    A person arrested without a warrant cannot be kept in custody for more than 24 hours without a Magistrate’s order, not counting travel time.Earlier law: CrPC 57

Chapter XIII: Information to the Police and Their Powers to InvestigateSections 173 to 196＋

How information about offences reaches the police, including the FIR, and the police powers of investigation.

Key sections explained

  • BNSS 173. Information in cognizable cases.
    The FIR provision. Information about a cognizable offence can be given orally or by electronic communication, whatever the place of the offence (this is the basis of the “Zero FIR”). An oral report is written down, read over and signed. A report sent electronically has to be signed within three days. Reports by women about certain offences are to be recorded by a woman officer.Earlier law: CrPC 154
  • BNSS 174. Information as to non-cognizable cases and investigation of such cases.
    Information about non-cognizable offences and how the police deal with them.Earlier law: CrPC 155
  • BNSS 175. Police officer's power to investigate cognizable case.
    The police officer’s power to investigate a cognizable case.Earlier law: CrPC 156
  • BNSS 176. Procedure for investigation.
    The officer must report to the Magistrate and visit the spot, with exceptions. In rape cases, the victim’s statement is to be recorded at her residence or place of choice, as far as possible by a woman officer, and it can be recorded by audio-video means.Earlier law: CrPC 157
  • BNSS 179. Police officer's power to require attendance of witnesses.
    The police officer’s power to require the attendance of witnesses.Earlier law: CrPC 160
  • BNSS 180. Examination of witnesses by police.
    Examination of witnesses by the police.Earlier law: CrPC 161
  • BNSS 183. Recording of confessions and statements.
    Recording of confessions and statements before a Magistrate.Earlier law: CrPC 164
  • BNSS 187. Procedure when investigation cannot be completed in twenty-four hours.
    When an investigation cannot be completed in 24 hours, the accused is sent to a Magistrate. The Magistrate can authorise police custody for up to 15 days in whole or in parts, within the first 40 or 60 days of the detention period, and detention overall is limited to 60 or 90 days depending on the offence. After that, the accused can claim default bail.Earlier law: CrPC 167
  • BNSS 193. Report of police officer on completion of investigation.
    The police report (chargesheet) on completing the investigation.Earlier law: CrPC 173
