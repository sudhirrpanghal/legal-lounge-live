Bare Act summaries

Plain-language summaries of 44 key sections from the Contract Act, the RTI Act and the Consumer Protection Act.

← Back to home

SectionHeadingIn plain languageAct
Section 2(a)ProposalOne person shows willingness to do or not do something in order to get the other’s assent.Contract Act
Section 2(b)PromiseA proposal, when accepted, becomes a promise.Contract Act
Section 2(d)ConsiderationSomething done, not done, or promised at the promisor’s desire, by the promisee or any other person.Contract Act
Section 2(e)AgreementEvery promise and every set of promises that form the consideration for each other.Contract Act
Section 2(g)Void agreementAn agreement that is not enforceable by law.Contract Act
Section 2(h)ContractAn agreement that is enforceable by law.Contract Act
Section 2(i)Voidable contractA contract enforceable at the option of one or more of the parties, but not of the other or others.Contract Act
Section 10What agreements are contractsAgreements made with free consent, by competent parties, for a lawful consideration and object, and not expressly declared void.Contract Act
Section 11Who is competent to contractA person who is of the age of majority, of sound mind and not disqualified by law.Contract Act
Section 14Free consentConsent is free when it is not caused by coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation or mistake.Contract Act
Section 15CoercionCommitting or threatening an act forbidden by law, or unlawfully detaining property, to make a person agree.Contract Act
Section 16Undue influenceOne party can dominate the other’s will and uses that position to obtain an unfair advantage.Contract Act
Section 17FraudDeceiving a party to induce a contract, for example by a knowingly false statement or by active concealment.Contract Act
Section 18MisrepresentationA false statement made innocently, or without reasonable grounds, that misleads the other party.Contract Act
Section 19, 19AVoidability where consent is not freeA contract caused by coercion, fraud or misrepresentation (Section 19) or undue influence (Section 19A) is voidable at the option of the affected party.Contract Act
Section 23Lawful consideration and objectUnlawful if forbidden by law, defeats a law, is fraudulent, injures a person or property, or is immoral or against public policy.Contract Act
Section 25Agreement without considerationVoid, with exceptions such as a written and registered agreement made out of natural love and affection, or a promise to pay a time-barred debt.Contract Act
Section 27Restraint of tradeAn agreement that restrains a person from carrying on a lawful profession, trade or business is void, subject to limited exceptions.Contract Act
Section 30Wager agreementsAgreements by way of wager are void.Contract Act
Section 56Impossible actsAn agreement to do an impossible act is void. A contract becomes void if the act later becomes impossible or unlawful.Contract Act
Section 62NovationThe parties may replace a contract with a new one, or alter or rescind it.Contract Act
Section 73Compensation for breachThe party who suffers a breach can claim compensation for loss that arose naturally or was in the parties’ contemplation. Remote loss is not covered.Contract Act
Section 74Named sum or penalty on breachWhere a sum is named as payable on breach, the party may receive reasonable compensation not exceeding that sum.Contract Act
Section 124, 126Indemnity and guaranteeIndemnity is a promise to protect against loss (Section 124). A guarantee is a promise to perform, or discharge the liability of, a third person if they default (Section 126).Contract Act
Section 3Right to informationSubject to the Act, all citizens have the right to information.RTI Act
Section 4Obligations of public authoritiesPublic authorities must maintain records and proactively disclose certain information.RTI Act
Section 5Public Information OfficersPublic authorities designate officers to receive and process requests.RTI Act
Section 6Making a requestApply in writing or electronically with the prescribed fee. The applicant does not have to give a reason (Section 6(2)).RTI Act
Section 7Disposal of a requestA decision is generally due within 30 days, or within 48 hours where the life or liberty of a person is involved.RTI Act
Section 8ExemptionsLists information that may be refused, such as matters affecting sovereignty and security. Some exemptions are subject to a public interest test (Section 8(2)).RTI Act
Section 11Third-party informationSets the procedure when the information relates to or was supplied by a third party.RTI Act
Section 18ComplaintsA person can complain to the Information Commission about certain failures of the authority.RTI Act
Section 19AppealsA first appeal goes to a senior officer within the authority (generally within 30 days). A second appeal goes to the Information Commission (generally within 90 days).RTI Act
Section 20PenaltiesThe Information Commission can impose penalties on a Public Information Officer in specified cases.RTI Act
Section 22Overriding effectThe Act has effect despite inconsistent provisions in other laws, such as the Official Secrets Act, 1923.RTI Act
Section 2(7)ConsumerA person who buys goods or hires or avails services for consideration, generally excluding those who buy for resale or commercial purposes.Consumer Protection Act
Section 2(9)Consumer rightsThe rights to safety, information, choice, being heard, seeking redress and consumer awareness.Consumer Protection Act
Section 2(10)DefectAny fault or imperfection in the quality, quantity, potency, purity or standard of goods.Consumer Protection Act
Section 2(11)DeficiencyAny fault or shortcoming in the quality, nature or manner of performance of a service.Consumer Protection Act
Section 2(47)Unfair trade practiceAn unfair or deceptive practice, such as a false representation or a misleading advertisement, in promoting the sale or supply of goods or services.Consumer Protection Act
Section 10Central Consumer Protection AuthorityEstablished to protect, promote and enforce consumer rights, including action against misleading advertisements.Consumer Protection Act
Section 35Filing a complaintA complaint is made to the District Commission, and it can be filed electronically.Consumer Protection Act
Section 69LimitationA complaint should generally be filed within two years from the date the cause of action arose. Delay can be condoned for sufficient reasons.Consumer Protection Act
Section 82 to 87Product liabilityManufacturers, sellers and service providers can be liable for harm caused by defective products or deficient services, subject to the conditions in the Act.Consumer Protection Act
