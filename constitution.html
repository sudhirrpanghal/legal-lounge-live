Constitution at a glance

A searchable quick reference to 179 key Articles of the Constitution of India, in plain language.

ArticleTitleIn plain languageGroup
PreamblePreamble to the Constitution of IndiaWe, the People of India, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic and to secure to all its citizens Justice, Liberty, Equality and Fraternity.Preamble
Article 1Name and territory of the UnionIndia, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States.The Union & Territory
Article 2Admission or establishment of new StatesParliament may by law admit into the Union, or establish, new States on such terms and conditions as it thinks fit.The Union & Territory
Article 3Formation of new States and alteration of areas, boundaries or names of existing StatesParliament may by law form a new State by separation of territory, alter boundaries, increase or diminish area, or change names of any State.The Union & Territory
Article 4Laws made under articles 2 and 3 to provide for the amendment of the First and the Fourth Schedules and supplemental, incidental and consequential mattersSupplemental, incidental and consequential provisions; such laws are not deemed to be amendments of the Constitution under Article 368.The Union & Territory
Article 5Citizenship at the commencement of the ConstitutionCitizenship for persons who had domicile in the territory of India and were born in India or whose parents were born in India, or resident for 5 years.The Union & Territory
Article 6Rights of citizenship of certain persons who have migrated to India from PakistanRights of persons who migrated from Pakistan before or after 19 July 1948 and registered as citizens.The Union & Territory
Article 7Rights of citizenship of certain migrants to PakistanPersons migrating to Pakistan after 1 March 1947 generally lost Indian citizenship, with exceptions for return under resettlement permits.The Union & Territory
Article 8Rights of citizenship of certain persons of Indian origin residing outside IndiaCitizenship for persons or ancestors born in undivided India residing abroad upon registration by diplomatic/consular representative.The Union & Territory
Article 9Persons voluntarily acquiring citizenship of a foreign State not to be citizensSingle citizenship principle: voluntary acquisition of foreign citizenship immediately terminates Indian citizenship.The Union & Territory
Article 10Continuance of the rights of citizenshipEvery citizen of India continues to be a citizen subject to the provisions of any law made by Parliament.The Union & Territory
Article 11Parliament to regulate the right of citizenship by lawParliament has full power to make provisions regarding the acquisition and termination of citizenship (Citizenship Act, 1955).The Union & Territory
Article 12Definition of State for Fundamental RightsIncludes the Government and Parliament of India, Government and Legislature of each State, and all local or other authorities within India.The Union & Territory
Article 13Laws inconsistent with or in derogation of the fundamental rightsPre-constitutional and post-constitutional laws conflicting with fundamental rights are void to the extent of inconsistency; judicial review.The Union & Territory
Article 14Equality before law and equal protection of the lawsThe State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws within the territory of India.The Union & Territory
Article 15Prohibition of discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birthProhibits discrimination; permits special provisions for women, children, SCs, STs, and socially and educationally backward classes.The Union & Territory
Article 16Equality of opportunity in matters of public employmentEqual opportunity in State employment; allows reservations for backward classes not adequately represented, SC/ST, and EWS.The Union & Territory
Article 17Abolition of UntouchabilityUntouchability is abolished and its practice in any form is forbidden and declared an offence punishable in accordance with law.The Union & Territory
Article 18Abolition of titlesProhibits State from conferring titles other than military or academic distinctions; citizens cannot accept foreign titles without President’s consent.The Union & Territory
Article 19Protection of certain rights regarding freedom of speech, assembly, association, movement, residence, and professionGuarantees six basic freedoms: speech & expression, peaceful assembly, forming associations/unions, free movement across India, residence, and trade/occupation, subject to reasonable restrictions.The Union & Territory
Article 20Protection in respect of conviction for offences (ex-post facto laws, double jeopardy, self-incrimination)Protects against ex-post facto criminal laws (20(1)), double jeopardy (20(2)), and self-incrimination (20(3)). Cannot be suspended in emergencies.The Union & Territory
Article 21Protection of life and personal libertyNo person shall be deprived of life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law; interpreted to include dignity, privacy, health, and fair trial.The Union & Territory
Article 21ARight to education (Free and compulsory education for children 6–14 years)Inserted by the 86th Amendment Act, 2002. Mandates the State to provide free and compulsory education to all children aged 6 to 14 years in such manner as the State may determine.The Union & Territory
Article 22Protection against arrest and detention in certain casesRight to be informed of grounds of arrest, consult an advocate, produced before magistrate within 24 hours; safeguards in preventive detention.The Union & Territory
Article 23Prohibition of traffic in human beings and forced labourProhibits human trafficking, begar and involuntary forced labour; violations punishable under law.The Union & Territory
Article 24Prohibition of employment of children in factories, mines and hazardous employmentNo child below the age of fourteen years shall be employed in any factory, mine or hazardous work.The Union & Territory
Article 25Freedom of conscience and free profession, practice and propagation of religionEqual entitlement to freedom of conscience and the right freely to profess, practise and propagate religion, subject to public order, morality and health.The Union & Territory
Article 26Freedom to manage religious affairsReligious denominations have right to establish and maintain institutions for religious/charitable purposes, manage own affairs, and own/acquire property.The Union & Territory
Article 27Freedom as to payment of taxes for promotion of any particular religionNo person compelled to pay taxes specifically appropriated for promotion or maintenance of any particular religion.The Union & Territory
Article 28Freedom as to attendance at religious instruction or religious worship in certain educational institutionsNo religious instruction in wholly state-funded institutions; no compulsion in state-recognised or state-aided schools without consent.The Union & Territory
Article 29Protection of interests of minoritiesAny section of citizens having a distinct language, script or culture has right to conserve the same; non-discrimination in admission to state-aided schools.The Union & Territory
Article 30Right of minorities to establish and administer educational institutionsAll minorities, whether based on religion or language, have the right to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice.The Union & Territory
Article 31Compulsory acquisition of property (Repealed by 44th Amendment Act, 1978)Omitted by the Constitution (Forty-fourth Amendment) Act, 1978; right to property moved to Article 300A as a legal/constitutional right.The Union & Territory
Article 31ASaving of laws providing for acquisition of estates, etc.Protects agrarian reform laws from challenges under Articles 14 and 19.The Union & Territory
Article 31BValidation of certain Acts and Regulations (Ninth Schedule immunity)Shields laws included in the Ninth Schedule from fundamental rights challenges; subject to basic structure review post-April 24, 1973 (I.R. Coelho).The Union & Territory
Article 31CSaving of laws giving effect to certain directive principlesProtects laws implementing Directive Principles in Article 39(b) and (c) from challenges under Articles 14 and 19.The Union & Territory
Article 32Remedies for enforcement of rights conferred by this Part (Right to Constitutional Remedies & Writs)Right to move Supreme Court by appropriate proceedings for enforcement of Part III rights; power to issue writs of Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Quo Warranto, Certiorari.The Union & Territory
Article 33Power of Parliament to modify the rights conferred by this Part in their application to Forces, etc.Parliament may modify or restrict fundamental rights for Armed Forces, police, intelligence agencies, and telecommunication systems.The Union & Territory
Article 34Restriction on rights conferred by this Part while martial law is in force in any areaParliament may indemnify any person for acts done in connection with maintenance or restoration of order in martial law areas.The Union & Territory
Article 35Legislation to give effect to the provisions of this PartParliament has exclusive power to prescribe punishment for offences against untouchability, human trafficking, and other fundamental rights violations.The Union & Territory
Article 36Definition of State for Directive PrinciplesSame meaning as in Part III (Article 12) unless the context otherwise requires.The Union & Territory
Article 37Application of the principles contained in this PartPrinciples are non-justiciable in any court, but fundamental in the governance of the country; duty of the State to apply these principles in making laws.The Union & Territory
Article 38State to secure a social order for the promotion of welfare of the peopleState shall strive to promote the welfare of the people by securing social, economic and political justice, and minimise inequalities in income, status and facilities.The Union & Territory
Article 39Certain principles of policy to be followed by the State (livelihood, distribution of resources, equal pay)Adequate means of livelihood, distribution of material resources to subserve common good, prevention of concentration of wealth, equal pay for equal work, health of workers and children.The Union & Territory
Article 39AEqual justice and free legal aidInserted by 42nd Amendment. Directs the State to provide free legal aid to ensure justice is not denied to any citizen by reason of economic or other disabilities. Foundation of NALSA Act, 1987.The Union & Territory
Article 40Organisation of village panchayatsState shall take steps to organise village panchayats and endow them with such powers and authority as may be necessary to enable them to function as units of self-government.The Union & Territory
Article 41Right to work, to education and to public assistance in certain casesState shall make effective provision for securing the right to work, education, and public assistance in unemployment, old age, sickness and disablement.The Union & Territory
Article 42Provision for just and humane conditions of work and maternity reliefState shall make provision for securing just and humane conditions of work and for maternity relief for women workers.The Union & Territory
Article 43Living wage, etc., for workersState shall endeavour to secure to all workers a living wage, conditions of work ensuring a decent standard of life, and full enjoyment of leisure and social/cultural opportunities.The Union & Territory
Article 43AParticipation of workers in management of industriesInserted by 42nd Amendment. Mandates steps by legislation to secure the participation of workers in the management of undertakings.The Union & Territory
Article 43BPromotion of co-operative societiesInserted by 97th Amendment Act, 2011. Directs State to promote voluntary formation, autonomous functioning, and democratic control of co-operative societies.The Union & Territory
Article 44Uniform civil code for the citizensThe State shall endeavour to secure for the citizens a Uniform Civil Code throughout the territory of India.The Union & Territory
Article 45Provision for early childhood care and education to children below the age of six yearsState shall endeavour to provide early childhood care and education for all children until they complete the age of six years.The Union & Territory
Article 46Promotion of educational and economic interests of Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and other weaker sectionsSpecial care for educational and economic interests of SCs, STs and weaker sections, and protection from social injustice and all forms of exploitation.The Union & Territory
Article 47Duty of the State to raise the level of nutrition and the standard of living and to improve public healthRegarded among primary duties; State shall endeavour to bring about prohibition of intoxicating drinks and drugs injurious to health.The Union & Territory
Article 48Organisation of agriculture and animal husbandryOrganising agriculture and animal husbandry on modern scientific lines; preserving and improving breeds and prohibiting slaughter of cows, calves and milch cattle.The Union & Territory
Article 48AProtection and improvement of environment and safeguarding of forests and wild lifeInserted by 42nd Amendment. Mandates that the State shall endeavour to protect and improve the environment and safeguard forests and wildlife of the country.The Union & Territory
Article 49Protection of monuments and places and objects of national importanceObligation of the State to protect every monument or place or object of artistic or historic interest declared of national importance from spoliation and disfigurement.The Union & Territory
Article 50Separation of judiciary from executiveThe State shall take steps to separate the judiciary from the executive in the public services of the State.The Union & Territory
Article 51Promotion of international peace and securityPromote international peace and security, maintain just relations between nations, foster respect for treaty obligations, and encourage settlement of international disputes by arbitration.The Union & Territory
Article 51AFundamental duties of citizens (Clauses a to k)Inserted by 42nd Amendment (and 86th Amendment for clause k). Prescribes 11 fundamental duties for every citizen of India, including abiding by the Constitution, protecting sovereignty, and striving towards excellence.The Union & Territory
Article 52The President of IndiaThere shall be a President of India, who is the head of the State and first citizen of India.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 53Executive power of the UnionVested in the President and exercised directly or through officers subordinate; supreme command of Defence Forces of the Union.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 54Election of PresidentElected by an electoral college consisting of elected members of both Houses of Parliament and elected members of Legislative Assemblies of States and UTs.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 55Manner of election of PresidentUniformity of representation among States and parity between Union and States; election held in accordance with proportional representation by single transferable vote.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 56Term of office of PresidentPresident holds office for a term of five years from the date of entering upon office; continues until successor enters upon office.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 57Eligibility for re-electionA person who holds, or who has held, office as President shall be eligible for re-election to that office.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 58Qualifications for election as PresidentCitizen of India, completed 35 years of age, qualified for election as member of Lok Sabha, and holds no office of profit.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 59Conditions of President's officeCannot be member of Parliament or State Legislature; official residence (Rashtrapati Bhavan) without rent; emoluments cannot be diminished during term.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 60Oath or affirmation by the PresidentAdministered by the Chief Justice of India, or senior-most Supreme Court Judge: preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 61Procedure for impeachment of the PresidentImpeachment for violation of the Constitution; charge preferred by either House by 1/4th members notice, passed by 2/3rd majority of total membership of each House.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 62Time of holding election to fill vacancy in the office of President and the term of office of person elected to fill casual vacancyElection to fill vacancy caused by expiration of term held before expiration; casual vacancy election held within six months.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 63The Vice-President of IndiaThere shall be a Vice-President of India.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 64The Vice-President to be ex officio Chairman of the Council of StatesEx-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha; shall not hold any other office of profit.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 65The Vice-President to act as President or to discharge his functions during casual vacancies in the office, or during the absence, of PresidentActs as President until new President enters upon office; receives President’s emoluments during such period.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 66Election of Vice-PresidentElected by electoral college of members of both Houses of Parliament by proportional representation; minimum 35 years of age; qualified for Rajya Sabha.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 67Term of office of Vice-PresidentTerm of five years; resignation addressed to President; removal by Rajya Sabha resolution passed by effective majority and agreed to by Lok Sabha.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 68Time of holding election to fill vacancy in the office of Vice-President and the term of office of person elected to fill casual vacancyElection to fill term expiry vacancy held before expiration; casual vacancy election held as soon as possible.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 69Oath or affirmation by the Vice-PresidentAdministered by the President or person appointed by him: bear true faith and allegiance to Constitution.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 70Discharge of President's functions in other contingenciesParliament may make such provision as it thinks fit for discharge of President’s functions in unprovided contingencies.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 71Matters relating to, or connected with, the election of a President or Vice-PresidentAll doubts and disputes inquired into and decided exclusively by the Supreme Court, whose decision shall be final.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 72Power of President to grant pardons, reprieves, respites or remissions of punishment or to suspend, remit or commute sentences in certain casesPardoning power in all court-martial cases, offences against Union laws, and all cases where sentence is a sentence of death.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 73Extent of executive power of the UnionCo-extensive with legislative power of Parliament (Union List & treaties); does not extend to State List matters save as expressly provided.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 74Council of Ministers to aid and advise PresidentCouncil of Ministers with Prime Minister at head to aid and advise President, who shall act in accordance with such advice; President may require one reconsideration.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 75Other provisions as to Ministers (appointment, tenure, collective responsibility)Prime Minister appointed by President; other Ministers appointed on PM advice; collective responsibility to Lok Sabha; Ministers hold office during President’s pleasure; 15% limit on Ministry size.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 76Attorney-General for IndiaAppointed by President; qualified to be appointed Supreme Court Judge; chief legal advisor to Government of India; right of audience in all courts in India.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 77Conduct of business of the Government of IndiaAll executive action expressed to be taken in the name of the President; President makes rules for allocation of government business.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 78Duties of Prime Minister as respects the furnishing of information to the President, etc.Communicate all decisions of Council of Ministers, furnish requested information, and submit matters for Council consideration on President’s request.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 79Constitution of ParliamentParliament consists of the President and two Houses: Council of States (Rajya Sabha) and House of the People (Lok Sabha).Union Executive & Parliament
Article 80Composition of the Council of States (Rajya Sabha)Up to 250 members: 12 nominated by President for literature, science, art, social service; up to 238 representatives of States and UTs elected by State MLAs.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 81Composition of the House of the People (Lok Sabha)Up to 550 members: up to 530 directly elected from territorial constituencies in States, and up to 20 from Union Territories.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 82Readjustment after each censusReadjustment of seats and territorial division into constituencies after each census by Delimitation Commission.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 83Duration of Houses of ParliamentRajya Sabha is not subject to dissolution (1/3rd members retire every second year); Lok Sabha term 5 years unless dissolved earlier.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 84Qualification for membership of ParliamentCitizen of India, minimum 30 years of age for Rajya Sabha and 25 years for Lok Sabha, and possessing qualifications prescribed by law.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 85Sessions of Parliament, prorogation and dissolutionPresident summons each House; maximum interval between two sessions is six months; President may prorogue Houses or dissolve Lok Sabha.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 86Right of President to address and send messages to HousesPresident may address either House or both assembled together, and send messages with respect to pending Bills.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 87Special address by the PresidentPresident addresses both Houses assembled together at commencement of first session after general election and first session of each year.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 88Rights of Ministers and Attorney-General as respects HousesRight to speak in and participate in proceedings of either House and joint sittings, but not entitled to vote unless a member.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 89The Chairman and Deputy Chairman of the Council of StatesVice-President is ex-officio Chairman; Rajya Sabha chooses one of its members to be Deputy Chairman.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 90Vacation and resignation of, and removal from, the office of Deputy ChairmanDeputy Chairman vacates office on ceasing to be member; resignation; removal by majority resolution of all members with 14 days notice.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 91Power of the Deputy Chairman or other person to perform the duties of the office of, or to act as, ChairmanDeputy Chairman acts when Chairman office is vacant or during Vice-President acting as President.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 92The Chairman or the Deputy Chairman not to preside while a resolution for his removal from office is under considerationBar on presiding during removal resolution; right to speak and take part, but no casting vote.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 93The Speaker and Deputy Speaker of the House of the PeopleLok Sabha chooses two of its members to be Speaker and Deputy Speaker.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 94Vacation and resignation of, and removal from, the offices of Speaker and Deputy SpeakerVacates office on ceasing to be member; resignation to Deputy Speaker/Speaker; removal by resolution of majority of all members; continues till first meeting of new House.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 95Power of the Deputy Speaker or other person to perform the duties of the office of, or to act as, SpeakerDeputy Speaker acts when Speaker office is vacant; Speaker pro tem appointed by President.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 96The Speaker or the Deputy Speaker not to preside while a resolution for his removal from office is under considerationSpeaker has right to speak and vote in first instance, but not casting vote in case of equality.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 97Salaries and allowances of the Chairman and Deputy Chairman and the Speaker and Deputy SpeakerFixed by Parliament by law and charged upon Consolidated Fund of India.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 98Secretariat of ParliamentEach House has separate secretarial staff; recruitment and conditions of service regulated by Parliament by law.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 99Oath or affirmation by members of ParliamentAdministered by President or person appointed: allegiance to Constitution and faithful discharge of duties.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 100Voting in Houses, power of Houses to act notwithstanding vacancies and quorumDecisions by majority of members present and voting; Speaker/Chairman has casting vote; quorum is 10% of total membership of House.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 101Vacation of seats in ParliamentProhibition of simultaneous membership of both Houses or in Parliament and State Legislature; resignation; continuous absence for 60 days without permission.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 102Disqualifications for membership of ParliamentOffice of profit, unsound mind, undischarged insolvent, foreign citizenship, and disqualification under Tenth Schedule (Anti-defection).Union Executive & Parliament
Article 103Decision on questions as to disqualifications of membersReferred to President whose decision is final; President obtains and acts according to opinion of Election Commission.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 104Penalty for sitting and voting before making oath or affirmation under article 99 or when not qualified or when disqualifiedPenalty of ₹500 for each day a person sits or votes without taking oath or when disqualified.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 105Powers, privileges, etc., of the Houses of Parliament and of the members and committees thereofFreedom of speech in Parliament; complete immunity from court proceedings for speech or vote given in Parliament or its committees.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 106Salaries and allowances of members of ParliamentDetermined by Parliament by law from time to time.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 107Provisions as to introduction and passing of BillsOrdinary Bills may originate in either House; must be agreed to by both Houses; prorogation does not lapse Bills, but dissolution of Lok Sabha lapses pending Bills.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 108Joint sitting of both Houses in certain casesPresident summons joint sitting in case of deadlock on ordinary Bill (rejection, disagreement on amendments, or 6 months delay); presided by Speaker of Lok Sabha.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 109Special procedure in respect of Money BillsMoney Bills introduced only in Lok Sabha on President’s recommendation; Rajya Sabha must return within 14 days with recommendations; Lok Sabha may accept or reject.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 110Definition of 'Money Bills'Deals exclusively with taxation, borrowing, custody of Consolidated/Contingency Fund, appropriation; Speaker’s certificate on Money Bill is final.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 111Assent to Bills by the PresidentPresident declares assent, withholds assent, or returns non-Money Bill for reconsideration; if passed again by both Houses, President must assent.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 112Annual financial statement (Union Budget)President causes statement of estimated receipts and expenditure of Government of India to be laid before both Houses for each financial year.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 113Procedure in Parliament with respect to estimatesExpenditure charged on Consolidated Fund not submitted to vote; other expenditure submitted to Lok Sabha as demands for grants; no demand without President’s recommendation.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 114Appropriation BillsBill introduced to provide for appropriation out of Consolidated Fund to meet voted grants and charged expenditure; no money withdrawn without Appropriation Act.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 115Supplementary, additional or excess grantsProvision for funds when amount authorized for service is insufficient or unexpected expenditure arises.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 116Votes on account, votes of credit and exceptional grantsVote on account enables government to incur expenditure pending passage of Appropriation Bill.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 117Special provisions as to financial BillsFinancial Bills under Art 110(1) introduced only in Lok Sabha with President’s recommendation.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 118Rules of procedure in ParliamentEach House of Parliament may make rules for regulating its procedure and conduct of business.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 119Regulation by law of procedure in Parliament in relation to financial businessParliament may regulate by law financial procedure to ensure timely completion of financial business.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 120Language to be used in ParliamentBusiness transacted in Hindi or English; Presiding officer may permit any member to address House in mother tongue.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 121Restriction on discussion in Parliament regarding conduct of judgesNo discussion in Parliament regarding conduct of Supreme Court or High Court Judges in discharge of duties, except upon impeachment motion.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 122Courts not to inquire into proceedings of ParliamentValidity of proceedings in Parliament cannot be called in question on ground of alleged irregularity of procedure.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 123Power of President to promulgate Ordinances during recess of ParliamentPromulgated when either House is not in session and immediate action required; has same force as Act of Parliament; ceases to operate 6 weeks from reassembly of Parliament.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 124Establishment and constitution of Supreme CourtSupreme Court consisting of Chief Justice of India and other Judges; appointment by President consulting collegium; tenure till age 65; removal by impeachment on proved misbehaviour/incapacity.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 124ANational Judicial Appointments Commission (Struck down in NJAC Case)Inserted by 99th Amendment Act, 2014, and struck down as unconstitutional by Supreme Court 5-judge bench in Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association (2015).Union Executive & Parliament
Article 125Salaries, etc., of Judges of the Supreme CourtDetermined by Parliament by law; charged on Consolidated Fund of India; cannot be varied to disadvantage after appointment.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 126Appointment of acting Chief JusticePresident appoints senior Judge to perform duties of CJI when office is vacant or CJI is unable to perform duties.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 127Appointment of ad hoc JudgesCJI with previous consent of President and consultation with Chief Justice of High Court may request High Court judge to attend Supreme Court sittings when quorum lacking.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 128Attendance of retired Judges at sittings of the Supreme CourtCJI with President’s consent may request retired Supreme Court or Federal Court Judge to sit and act as Judge.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 129Supreme Court to be a court of recordCourt of record with all powers of such court including power to punish for contempt of itself.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 130Seat of Supreme CourtSupreme Court sits in Delhi or such other place as Chief Justice of India may, with approval of President, appoint.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 131Original jurisdiction of the Supreme CourtExclusive original jurisdiction in legal disputes between Government of India and States, or between States.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 132Appellate jurisdiction of Supreme Court in appeals from High Courts in certain casesAppeal lies on certificate under Art 134A that case involves substantial question of law as to interpretation of Constitution.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 133Appellate jurisdiction of Supreme Court in appeals from High Courts in regard to civil mattersAppeal from High Court on certificate that case involves substantial question of law of general importance needing Supreme Court decision.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 134Appellate jurisdiction of Supreme Court in regard to criminal mattersAppeal lies where High Court has reversed acquittal and sentenced accused to death/life, or certified case fit for appeal.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 135Jurisdiction and powers of the Federal Court under existing law to be exercisable by the Supreme CourtSupreme Court exercises jurisdiction formerly exercisable by Federal Court in matters not covered by Articles 133 and 134.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 136Special leave to appeal by the Supreme Court (SLP)Supreme Court may grant special leave to appeal from any judgment, decree, sentence or order in any cause or matter passed by any court or tribunal in India.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 137Review of judgments or orders by the Supreme CourtSupreme Court has power to review any judgment pronounced or order made by it (curative petition/review petition).Union Executive & Parliament
Article 138Enlargement of the jurisdiction of the Supreme CourtParliament may by law confer further jurisdiction with respect to matters in Union List or agreed by States.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 139Conferment on the Supreme Court of powers to issue certain writsParliament may by law empower Supreme Court to issue directions or writs for purposes other than enforcement of fundamental rights.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 140Ancillary powers of Supreme CourtParliament may by law confer supplemental powers on Supreme Court not inconsistent with Constitution to enable effective exercise of jurisdiction.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 141Law declared by Supreme Court to be binding on all courts within the territory of IndiaThe law declared by the Supreme Court shall be binding on all courts within the territory of India (doctrine of binding precedent).Union Executive & Parliament
Article 142Enforcement of decrees and orders of Supreme Court and orders as to discovery, etc. (complete justice)Supreme Court may pass decrees or orders necessary for doing complete justice in any cause or matter; enforceable throughout India.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 143Power of President to consult Supreme Court (Advisory Jurisdiction)President may refer any question of law or fact of public importance to Supreme Court for opinion.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 144Civil and judicial authorities to act in aid of the Supreme CourtAll authorities, civil and judicial, in the territory of India shall act in aid of the Supreme Court.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 145Rules of Court, etc.Supreme Court makes rules for regulating practice and procedure of Court with approval of President; minimum 5 judges for Constitution Bench.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 146Officers and servants and the expenses of the Supreme CourtAppointments made by Chief Justice of India; administrative expenses charged upon Consolidated Fund of India.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 147Interpretation of constitutional questionsReferences to interpretation of Constitution include interpretation of Government of India Act, 1935 and Indian Independence Act, 1947.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 148Comptroller and Auditor-General of IndiaAppointed by President by warrant; removed only in like manner and on like grounds as Supreme Court Judge; independent constitutional auditor.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 149Duties and powers of the Comptroller and Auditor-GeneralPerforms duties and exercises powers in relation to accounts of Union and of States as prescribed by law (CAG Act, 1971).Union Executive & Parliament
Article 150Form of accounts of the Union and of the StatesAccounts kept in such form as President may, on advice of CAG, prescribe.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 151Audit reports of the Comptroller and Auditor-GeneralCAG reports relating to Union accounts submitted to President, who causes them to be laid before Parliament; State reports laid before State Legislatures.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 152Definition of 'State' in Part VIExcludes Jammu and Kashmir historically (now integrated as Union Territory).Union Executive & Parliament
Article 153Governors of StatesThere shall be a Governor for each State; same person may be appointed Governor for two or more States.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 154Executive power of StateExecutive power of State vested in Governor and exercised directly or through subordinate officers.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 155Appointment of GovernorGovernor appointed by President by warrant under hand and seal.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 156Term of office of GovernorHolds office during pleasure of President; subject to that, holds office for term of five years.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 157Qualifications for appointment as GovernorCitizen of India and completed age of thirty-five years.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 158Conditions of Governor's officeShall not be member of Parliament or Legislature; shall not hold any other office of profit; emoluments cannot be diminished during term.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 159Oath or affirmation by the GovernorAdministered by Chief Justice of High Court: preserve, protect and defend Constitution and law.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 160Discharge of the functions of the Governor in certain contingenciesPresident may make provision for discharge of functions in unprovided contingencies.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 161Power of Governor to grant pardons, reprieves, respites or remissions of punishmentPower to grant pardons, reprieves, respites or remissions for offences against laws relating to matter within State executive power.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 162Extent of executive power of StateCo-extensive with legislative power of State Legislature (State List and Concurrent List).Union Executive & Parliament
Article 163Council of Ministers to aid and advise GovernorCouncil of Ministers with Chief Minister at head to aid and advise Governor, except where Governor acts in discretion.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 164Other provisions as to Ministers (appointment, tenure, salaries)Chief Minister appointed by Governor; other Ministers appointed on CM advice; collective responsibility to Legislative Assembly; 15% limit.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 165Advocate-General for the StateAppointed by Governor; qualified to be High Court Judge; advises State Government on legal matters; audience in all courts in State.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 166Conduct of business of the Government of a StateExecutive action expressed to be taken in name of Governor; Governor makes rules for allocation of business.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 167Duties of Chief Minister as respects the furnishing of information to Governor, etc.Communicate all Cabinet decisions, supply requested administrative information, and submit matters for Cabinet consideration.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 168Constitution of Legislatures in StatesConsists of Governor and one or two Houses (Legislative Assembly and Legislative Council in specified States).Union Executive & Parliament
Article 169Abolition or creation of Legislative Councils in StatesParliament may by law abolish or create Legislative Council if State Assembly passes resolution by 2/3rd majority present and voting.Union Executive & Parliament
