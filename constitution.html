Constitution at a glance

A searchable quick reference to 179 key Articles of the Constitution of India, in plain language.

← Back to home

ArticleTitleIn plain languageGroup
PreamblePreamble to the Constitution of IndiaWe, the People of India, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic and to secure to all its citizens Justice, Liberty, Equality and Fraternity.Preamble
Article 1Name and territory of the UnionIndia, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States.The Union & Territory
Article 2Admission or establishment of new StatesParliament may by law admit into the Union, or establish, new States on such terms as it thinks fit.The Union & Territory
Article 3Formation of new States and alteration of areas, boundaries or names of existing StatesParliament may form a new State, alter the boundary or area, or change the name of any State by law.The Union & Territory
Article 4Laws made under Articles 2 and 3 to provide for amendment of First and Fourth SchedulesSuch laws are not deemed to be constitutional amendments under Article 368.The Union & Territory
Article 5Citizenship at the commencement of the ConstitutionCitizenship for persons domiciled in India who were born in India or whose parents were born in India.Citizenship
Article 6Rights of citizenship of persons who have migrated to India from PakistanCitizenship rights for migrants from Pakistan before or after July 19, 1948.Citizenship
Article 7Rights of citizenship of certain migrants to PakistanPersons migrating to Pakistan after March 1, 1947 generally ceased to be citizens of India.Citizenship
Article 8Rights of citizenship of persons of Indian origin residing outside IndiaRegistration as citizens through Indian diplomatic or consular representatives.Citizenship
Article 9Persons voluntarily acquiring citizenship of a foreign State not to be citizensSingle citizenship rule: acquiring foreign citizenship terminates Indian citizenship.Citizenship
Article 10Continuance of the rights of citizenshipCitizenship continues subject to laws made by Parliament.Citizenship
Article 11Parliament to regulate the right of citizenship by lawParliament has sovereign authority to regulate citizenship acquisition and termination (Citizenship Act, 1955).Citizenship
Article 12Definition of “State”For Part III, “the State” includes Government and Parliament of India, State Governments and Legislatures, and all local or other authorities.Fundamental Rights
Article 13Laws inconsistent with or in derogation of Fundamental RightsLaws violating fundamental rights are void to the extent of inconsistency; establishes judicial review.Fundamental Rights
Article 14Equality before lawThe State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or equal protection of the laws within the territory of India.Fundamental Rights
Article 15Prohibition of discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birthProhibits discrimination; permits special provisions for women, children, SC/ST, and socially/educationally backward classes.Fundamental Rights
Article 16Equality of opportunity in matters of public employmentEqual opportunity in State jobs; allows reservations for backward classes not adequately represented and EWS.Fundamental Rights
Article 17Abolition of UntouchabilityUntouchability is abolished and its practice in any form is forbidden and punishable by law.Fundamental Rights
Article 18Abolition of titlesNo title, not being a military or academic distinction, shall be conferred by the State.Fundamental Rights
Article 19(1)(a)Freedom of speech and expressionRight to express views freely via speech, writing, printing, or media, subject to reasonable restrictions under Article 19(2).Fundamental Rights
Article 19(1)(b)Right to assemble peaceably and without armsRight of citizens to hold peaceful, unarmed public gatherings and protests.Fundamental Rights
Article 19(1)(c)Right to form associations, unions or co-operative societiesRight to form trade unions, clubs, societies, and political parties.Fundamental Rights
Article 19(1)(d)Right to move freely throughout the territory of IndiaRight to travel anywhere across India without inter-state barriers.Fundamental Rights
Article 19(1)(e)Right to reside and settle in any part of the territory of IndiaRight to live and settle anywhere in India, subject to reasonable restrictions for tribal areas.Fundamental Rights
Article 19(1)(g)Right to practise any profession, or to carry on any occupation, trade or businessRight to work and trade, subject to state qualifications and state monopolies.Fundamental Rights
Article 20(1)Protection against ex-post facto criminal lawsNo person convicted of an offence except for violation of law in force at time of commission, nor given greater penalty.Fundamental Rights
Article 20(2)Protection against double jeopardyNo person shall be prosecuted and punished for the same offence more than once.Fundamental Rights
Article 20(3)Protection against self-incriminationNo person accused of any offence shall be compelled to be a witness against himself.Fundamental Rights
Article 21Protection of life and personal libertyNo person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law; includes dignity, privacy, and fair trial.Fundamental Rights
Article 21ARight to educationThe State shall provide free and compulsory education to all children of the age of six to fourteen years.Fundamental Rights
Article 22Protection against arrest and detention in certain casesRight to be informed of grounds of arrest, consult a lawyer, produced before magistrate within 24 hours; limits preventive detention.Fundamental Rights
Article 23Prohibition of traffic in human beings and forced labourTraffic in human beings, begar and other similar forms of forced labour are prohibited.Fundamental Rights
Article 24Prohibition of employment of children in factories, etc.No child below the age of fourteen years shall be employed in any factory or mine or engaged in hazardous employment.Fundamental Rights
Article 25Freedom of conscience and free profession, practice and propagation of religionAll persons are equally entitled to freedom of conscience and the right freely to profess, practise and propagate religion.Fundamental Rights
Article 26Freedom to manage religious affairsReligious denominations have right to establish and maintain institutions and manage their own affairs in matters of religion.Fundamental Rights
Article 27Freedom as to payment of taxes for promotion of any particular religionNo person compelled to pay taxes the proceeds of which are specifically appropriated for promoting any religion.Fundamental Rights
Article 28Freedom as to attendance at religious instruction in educational institutionsNo religious instruction in wholly state-funded educational institutions.Fundamental Rights
Article 29Protection of interests of minoritiesAny section of citizens having a distinct language, script or culture has the right to conserve the same.Fundamental Rights
Article 30Right of minorities to establish and administer educational institutionsAll minorities, whether based on religion or language, have the right to establish and administer educational institutions.Fundamental Rights
Article 31A-31CSaving of certain laws protecting agrarian reforms and Directive PrinciplesConstitutional immunities for land acquisition laws and laws giving effect to Articles 39(b) and 39(c).Fundamental Rights
Article 32Remedies for enforcement of Fundamental Rights (Right to Constitutional Remedies)Right to move the Supreme Court by appropriate proceedings; power to issue writs of Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Quo Warranto, and Certiorari.Fundamental Rights
Article 33Power of Parliament to modify Fundamental Rights in application to Armed ForcesParliament may restrict rights for armed forces, intelligence agencies and police to ensure proper discharge of duties.Fundamental Rights
Article 34Restriction on Fundamental Rights while martial law is in forceIndemnifying state servants for acts done in connection with maintenance of order during martial law.Fundamental Rights
Article 35Legislation to give effect to the provisions of Part IIIParliament alone has power to make laws prescribing punishments for violations of fundamental rights.Fundamental Rights
Article 36-37Definition and application of Directive PrinciplesPrinciples are non-justiciable in court, but fundamental in the governance of the country; duty of the State to apply them in making laws.Directive Principles & Duties
Article 38State to secure a social order for the promotion of welfare of the peoplePromoting welfare by securing social, economic and political justice, and minimizing inequalities.Directive Principles & Duties
Article 39Certain principles of policy to be followed by the StateAdequate means of livelihood, distribution of material resources to subserve common good, preventing concentration of wealth, equal pay for equal work.Directive Principles & Duties
Article 39AEqual justice and free legal aidThe State shall provide free legal aid by suitable legislation or schemes to ensure opportunities for securing justice are not denied by economic disability.Directive Principles & Duties
Article 40Organisation of village panchayatsState shall take steps to organise village panchayats and endow them with powers as units of self-government.Directive Principles & Duties
Article 41Right to work, to education and to public assistance in certain casesEffective provision for securing right to work, education and relief in unemployment, old age, sickness and disablement.Directive Principles & Duties
Article 42Provision for just and humane conditions of work and maternity reliefState shall make provision for securing just and humane conditions of work and for maternity relief.Directive Principles & Duties
Article 43Living wage, etc., for workersState shall endeavour to secure a living wage, decent standard of life and social and cultural opportunities for all workers.Directive Principles & Duties
Article 43AParticipation of workers in management of industriesSteps to secure the participation of workers in the management of undertakings and establishments.Directive Principles & Duties
Article 43BPromotion of co-operative societiesState shall endeavour to promote voluntary formation, autonomous functioning, and democratic control of co-operative societies.Directive Principles & Duties
Article 44Uniform Civil Code for the citizensThe State shall endeavour to secure for the citizens a Uniform Civil Code throughout the territory of India.Directive Principles & Duties
Article 45Provision for early childhood care and education to children below six yearsEndeavour to provide early childhood care and education for all children until they complete the age of six years.Directive Principles & Duties
Article 46Promotion of educational and economic interests of SC, ST and other weaker sectionsSpecial care for educational and economic interests of Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and protection from social injustice.Directive Principles & Duties
Article 47Duty of the State to raise the level of nutrition and standard of living and public healthRegarded among primary duties; prohibition of intoxicating drinks and drugs injurious to health.Directive Principles & Duties
Article 48Organisation of agriculture and animal husbandryOrganising agriculture on modern scientific lines; preserving and improving breeds and prohibiting slaughter of cows and calves.Directive Principles & Duties
Article 48AProtection and improvement of environment and safeguarding of forests and wildlifeThe State shall endeavour to protect and improve the environment and safeguard the forests and wild life of the country.Directive Principles & Duties
Article 49Protection of monuments and places and objects of national importanceObligation of the State to protect every monument or place of historic or artistic interest from spoliation, destruction or removal.Directive Principles & Duties
Article 50Separation of judiciary from executiveThe State shall take steps to separate the judiciary from the executive in the public services of the State.Directive Principles & Duties
Article 51Promotion of international peace and securityPromote international peace, maintain just relations between nations, foster respect for treaty obligations, and encourage arbitration of international disputes.Directive Principles & Duties
Article 51AFundamental Duties of citizens of India11 duties: abide by Constitution, cherish freedom struggle ideals, uphold sovereignty, defend nation, promote harmony, preserve heritage, protect environment, develop scientific temper, safeguard public property, strive for excellence, and provide educational opportunities to child.Directive Principles & Duties
Article 52The President of IndiaThere shall be a President of India.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 53Executive power of the UnionVested in the President and exercised directly or through subordinate officers; supreme command of Defence Forces.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 54-55Election and manner of election of PresidentElected by electoral college of elected members of both Houses of Parliament and Legislative Assemblies of States via proportional representation.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 56Term of office of PresidentHolds office for five years from date of entering upon office.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 61Procedure for impeachment of the PresidentImpeachment for violation of the Constitution by 2/3rd majority of total membership in both Houses of Parliament.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 63The Vice-President of IndiaThere shall be a Vice-President of India.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 64Vice-President to be ex-officio Chairman of the Council of StatesEx-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha; receives no salary as Vice-President.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 72Power of President to grant pardons, reprieves, respites or remissions of punishmentPardoning power in all cases of court martial, offences against Union laws, and death sentences.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 74Council of Ministers to aid and advise PresidentCouncil of Ministers with Prime Minister at head to aid and advise President, who shall act in accordance with such advice; reconsideration permitted once.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 75Other provisions as to MinistersPrime Minister appointed by President; Ministers collectively responsible to House of the People (Lok Sabha); size capped at 15% of Lok Sabha.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 76Attorney-General for IndiaAppointed by President; must be qualified to be appointed Supreme Court Judge; chief legal adviser to Government of India; right of audience in all courts.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 77-78Conduct of business of Government of India and duties of Prime MinisterAll executive actions taken in name of President; duty of PM to communicate all decisions of Council of Ministers to President.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 79Constitution of ParliamentParliament consists of President and two Houses: Council of States (Rajya Sabha) and House of the People (Lok Sabha).Union Executive & Parliament
Article 80Composition of the Council of States (Rajya Sabha)Up to 250 members: 12 nominated by President for literature, science, art, social service; up to 238 representatives of States and UTs.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 81Composition of the House of the People (Lok Sabha)Up to 550 members elected by direct election from territorial constituencies.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 83Duration of Houses of ParliamentRajya Sabha is permanent body not subject to dissolution (1/3rd retire every 2 years); Lok Sabha 5 years unless dissolved earlier.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 85Sessions of Parliament, prorogation and dissolutionPresident summons Parliament; interval between two sessions cannot exceed six months.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 102Disqualifications for membership of ParliamentOffice of profit, unsound mind, undischarged insolvent, foreign citizenship, or disqualified under Tenth Schedule (Anti-defection).Union Executive & Parliament
Article 105Powers, privileges, etc., of the Houses of Parliament and of the membersFreedom of speech in Parliament; immunity from court proceedings for anything said or vote given in Parliament.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 108Joint sitting of both Houses in certain casesJoint sitting summoned by President in case of deadlock on ordinary bill; presided by Speaker of Lok Sabha.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 109-110Special procedure and definition of “Money Bills”Money Bills introduced only in Lok Sabha on President’s recommendation; Rajya Sabha has 14 days to suggest changes; Speaker’s certificate is final.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 112Annual financial statement (Union Budget)President causes statement of estimated receipts and expenditure to be laid before both Houses for every financial year.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 123Power of President to promulgate Ordinances during recess of ParliamentPresident may promulgate Ordinances when either House not in session and immediate action required; must be approved by Parliament within 6 weeks of reassembly.Union Executive & Parliament
Article 124Establishment and constitution of Supreme CourtSupreme Court consisting of Chief Justice of India and other Judges; appointment by President consulting collegium; tenure till age 65; removal on proved misbehaviour/incapacity.Union Judiciary (Supreme Court)
Article 125Salaries, etc., of JudgesCharged on Consolidated Fund of India; cannot be varied to disadvantage after appointment.Union Judiciary (Supreme Court)
Article 126Appointment of acting Chief JusticePresident appoints senior Judge to perform duties when CJI office vacant.Union Judiciary (Supreme Court)
Article 127Appointment of ad hoc JudgesCJI appoints High Court judge as ad hoc Supreme Court judge with President’s consent when quorum lacking.Union Judiciary (Supreme Court)
Article 129Supreme Court to be a court of recordCourt of record with all powers including power to punish for contempt of itself.Union Judiciary (Supreme Court)
Article 130Seat of Supreme CourtSits in Delhi or such other place as CJI may, with approval of President, appoint.Union Judiciary (Supreme Court)
Article 131Original jurisdiction of the Supreme CourtExclusive jurisdiction in inter-governmental disputes between Centre and States or between States involving legal rights.Union Judiciary (Supreme Court)
Article 132Appellate jurisdiction of Supreme Court in constitutional appealsAppeal lies from High Court on certificate that case involves substantial question of law as to interpretation of Constitution.Union Judiciary (Supreme Court)
Article 133Appellate jurisdiction in civil mattersCivil appeal from High Court on substantial question of law of general importance needing Supreme Court decision.Union Judiciary (Supreme Court)
Article 134Appellate jurisdiction in criminal mattersCriminal appeal from High Court where death sentence awarded on reversing acquittal or case certified fit for appeal.Union Judiciary (Supreme Court)
Article 136Special leave to appeal by the Supreme Court (SLP)Supreme Court may grant special leave to appeal from any judgment, decree, sentence or order in any cause or matter by any court or tribunal in India.Union Judiciary (Supreme Court)
Article 137Review of judgments or orders by the Supreme CourtSupreme Court has power to review any judgment pronounced or order made by it.Union Judiciary (Supreme Court)
Article 139ATransfer of certain casesSupreme Court power to transfer cases involving same questions of law from High Courts to itself, or from one High Court to another.Union Judiciary (Supreme Court)
Article 141Law declared by Supreme Court to be binding on all courtsThe law declared by the Supreme Court shall be binding on all courts within the territory of India (doctrine of precedent).Union Judiciary (Supreme Court)
Article 142Enforcement of decrees and orders; Complete justice powerSupreme Court may pass such decree or make such order as is necessary for doing complete justice in any cause or matter pending before it.Union Judiciary (Supreme Court)
Article 143Power of President to consult Supreme Court (Advisory Jurisdiction)President may refer question of law or fact of public importance to Supreme Court for opinion.Union Judiciary (Supreme Court)
Article 144Civil and judicial authorities to act in aid of the Supreme CourtAll authorities, civil and judicial, in the territory of India shall act in aid of the Supreme Court.Union Judiciary (Supreme Court)
Article 148-151Comptroller and Auditor-General of India (CAG)Auditor-General appointed by President; audits accounts of Union and States; reports submitted to President/Governor.Union Judiciary (Supreme Court)
Article 153-154Governors of States and Executive power of StateGovernor for each State; executive power of State vested in Governor.States & High Courts
Article 155-156Appointment and term of office of GovernorAppointed by President by warrant under hand and seal; holds office during pleasure of President.States & High Courts
Article 161Power of Governor to grant pardons, reprieves, etc.Pardoning power for offences against laws relating to matters within executive power of State.States & High Courts
Article 163Council of Ministers to aid and advise GovernorCouncil of Ministers with Chief Minister at head to aid and advise Governor, except where Governor acts in discretion.States & High Courts
Article 164Other provisions as to Ministers in StatesChief Minister appointed by Governor; Ministers collectively responsible to Legislative Assembly.States & High Courts
Article 165Advocate-General for the StateAppointed by Governor; qualified to be High Court Judge; advises State Government on legal matters.States & High Courts
Article 168-170Constitution and composition of State LegislaturesLegislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha) and Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad) in certain States.States & High Courts
Article 200Assent to Bills by GovernorGovernor assents, withholds assent, returns bill for reconsideration, or reserves bill for consideration of President.States & High Courts
Article 213Power of Governor to promulgate Ordinances during recess of LegislatureGovernor ordinance power when Legislature not in session; valid for 6 weeks from reassembly.States & High Courts
Article 214High Courts for StatesThere shall be a High Court for each State.States & High Courts
Article 215High Courts to be courts of recordCourt of record with all powers including power to punish for contempt of itself.States & High Courts
Article 216-217Constitution and appointment of High Court JudgesAppointed by President after consultation with CJI, Governor, and Chief Justice of High Court; holds office till age 62.States & High Courts
Article 226Power of High Courts to issue certain writsHigh Court power to issue writs (Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Quo Warranto, Certiorari) for enforcement of Fundamental Rights and for any other legal right.States & High Courts
Article 227Power of superintendence over all courts by the High CourtSuperintendence over all subordinate courts and tribunals throughout territories in relation to which it exercises jurisdiction.States & High Courts
Article 231Establishment of a common High Court for two or more StatesParliament may by law establish a common High Court for two or more States or UTs (e.g., Punjab & Haryana High Court).States & High Courts
Article 233-235Subordinate Courts and District JudgesAppointment of District Judges by Governor in consultation with High Court; control over subordinate courts vested in High Court.States & High Courts
Article 239Administration of Union TerritoriesAdministered by President acting through Administrator or Lieutenant Governor.Panchayats, Municipalities & UTs
Article 239AASpecial provisions with respect to Delhi (National Capital Territory)Legislative Assembly for NCT of Delhi; Council of Ministers headed by CM; powers over State List except Police, Public Order and Land.Panchayats, Municipalities & UTs
Article 243-243OThe Panchayats (Part IX - 73rd Amendment)Three-tier Panchayati Raj institutions (Gram, Intermediate, District); reservations for women (minimum 1/3rd) and SC/ST; State Finance Commission and State Election Commission.Panchayats, Municipalities & UTs
Article 243P-243ZGThe Municipalities (Part IXA - 74th Amendment)Nagar Panchayats, Municipal Councils, Municipal Corporations; Ward committees; urban local self-government.Panchayats, Municipalities & UTs
Article 243ZH-243ZTThe Co-operative Societies (Part IXB - 97th Amendment)Incorporation, regulation and democratic governance of co-operative societies.Panchayats, Municipalities & UTs
Article 245Extent of laws made by Parliament and by Legislatures of StatesParliament makes laws for whole or any part of India; State Legislature for whole or part of State; Parliament has extra-territorial competence.Centre-State Relations & Finance
Article 246Subject-matter of laws made by Parliament and State Legislatures (Seventh Schedule)Division of powers: List I (Union List - 100 subjects), List II (State List - 61 subjects), List III (Concurrent List - 52 subjects).Centre-State Relations & Finance
Article 246ASpecial provision with respect to Goods and Services Tax (GST)Simultaneous power of Parliament and State Legislatures to make laws with respect to GST; Parliament has exclusive power on inter-state supply.Centre-State Relations & Finance
Article 248Residuary powers of legislationParliament has exclusive power to make any law with respect to any matter not enumerated in Concurrent List or State List.Centre-State Relations & Finance
Article 249Power of Parliament to legislate in national interest on State ListRajya Sabha resolution supported by not less than 2/3rd members present and voting empowers Parliament.Centre-State Relations & Finance
Article 250Power of Parliament to legislate on State List during EmergencyParliament has power to make laws on State List while Proclamation of Emergency is in operation.Centre-State Relations & Finance
Article 254Inconsistency between laws made by Parliament and laws made by State LegislaturesDoctrine of Repugnancy: Union law prevails over State law in Concurrent List; State law prevails if reserved for and received President’s assent.Centre-State Relations & Finance
Article 256-257Obligation of States and Union control over StatesExecutive power of State exercised to ensure compliance with laws made by Parliament; Union directions for communications and railways.Centre-State Relations & Finance
Article 262Adjudication of disputes relating to waters of inter-State rivers or river valleysParliament may by law provide for adjudication of inter-State water disputes; jurisdiction of Supreme Court barred (Inter-State River Water Disputes Act).Centre-State Relations & Finance
Article 263Provisions with respect to an Inter-State CouncilPresident may establish Inter-State Council to inquire into and advise on disputes between States and promote coordination.Centre-State Relations & Finance
Article 265Taxes not to be imposed save by authority of lawNo tax shall be levied or collected except by authority of law.Centre-State Relations & Finance
Article 266Consolidated Funds and Public Accounts of India and of the StatesAll revenues, loans and money received flow into Consolidated Fund; withdrawn only by parliamentary appropriation.Centre-State Relations & Finance
Article 267Contingency FundEstablished by Parliament/State Legislature at disposal of President/Governor to meet unforeseen expenditure pending authorisation.Centre-State Relations & Finance
Article 279AGoods and Services Tax Council (GST Council)Joint forum of Centre and States constituted by President; recommends tax rates, exemptions, thresholds; chaired by Union Finance Minister.Centre-State Relations & Finance
Article 280Finance CommissionConstituted by President every 5 years; recommends devolution of net tax proceeds between Union and States and grants-in-aid.Centre-State Relations & Finance
Article 299Contracts of the Union and the StatesGovernment contracts must be expressed to be made by President or Governor and executed by authorized persons.Centre-State Relations & Finance
Article 300Suits and proceedings (Government liability)Government of India may sue or be sued by the name of the Union of India; State may sue or be sued in name of State.Centre-State Relations & Finance
Article 300APersons not to be deprived of property save by authority of law (Right to Property)No person shall be deprived of his property save by authority of law (moved from Part III to constitutional/human right by 44th Amendment).Centre-State Relations & Finance
Article 301Freedom of trade, commerce and intercourseTrade, commerce and intercourse throughout territory of India shall be free, subject to parliamentary restrictions in public interest.Trade, Commerce & Services
Article 302-304Power of Parliament and States to impose restrictions on tradeParliament may restrict trade in public interest; States may impose non-discriminatory taxes and reasonable restrictions with President’s sanction.Trade, Commerce & Services
Article 309-310Recruitment and tenure of services (Doctrine of Pleasure)Civil servants hold office during pleasure of President or Governor, subject to constitutional safeguards.Trade, Commerce & Services
Article 311Dismissal, removal or reduction in rank of civil servantsNo dismissal by authority subordinate to appointing authority; mandatory reasonable opportunity of hearing (inquiry) before dismissal.Trade, Commerce & Services
Article 312All-India ServicesRajya Sabha resolution by 2/3rd majority empowers Parliament to create new All-India Services common to Union and States (IAS, IPS, IFS).Trade, Commerce & Services
Article 315-323Public Service Commissions (UPSC and State PSCs)Independent constitutional commissions for appointments to Union and State civil services; annual reports to President/Governor.Trade, Commerce & Services
Article 323AAdministrative TribunalsParliament may establish Administrative Tribunals for disputes concerning recruitment and service conditions (e.g., Central Administrative Tribunal - CAT).Trade, Commerce & Services
Article 323BTribunals for other mattersEstablishment of tribunals for taxes, foreign exchange, industrial disputes, land reforms, rent, and elections.Trade, Commerce & Services
Article 324Superintendence, direction and control of elections in an Election CommissionIndependent Election Commission of India responsible for conducting free and fair elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, and offices of President and VP.Elections & Special Provisions
Article 325No person to be ineligible for inclusion in electoral roll on grounds of religion, race, caste or sexSingle general electoral roll for every territorial constituency without discrimination.Elections & Special Provisions
Article 326Elections to Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies on basis of adult suffrageUniversal adult suffrage: every citizen not less than 18 years entitled to vote unless disqualified by law for unsoundness of mind, crime, or corrupt practice.Elections & Special Provisions
Article 329Bar to interference by courts in electoral mattersValidity of law relating to delimitation or allotment of seats not questioned; election challenged only by election petition.Elections & Special Provisions
Article 330-332Reservation of seats for SC and ST in Lok Sabha and Legislative AssembliesSeats reserved in proportion to population in each State and Union Territory.Elections & Special Provisions
Article 335Claims of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes to services and postsClaims taken into consideration consistently with maintenance of efficiency of administration.Elections & Special Provisions
Article 338National Commission for Scheduled Castes (NCSC)Constitutional commission to investigate and monitor all matters relating to safeguards provided for SCs.Elections & Special Provisions
Article 338ANational Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST)Constitutional commission investigating safeguards for STs; reports directly to President.Elections & Special Provisions
Article 338BNational Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC)Constitutional commission for Socially and Educationally Backward Classes (SEBCs) added by 102nd Amendment.Elections & Special Provisions
Article 343Official language of the UnionHindi in Devanagari script is the official language of the Union; international form of Indian numerals; English continued by statute.Official Language
Article 345Official language or languages of a StateState Legislature may adopt one or more languages used in State or Hindi for official purposes.Official Language
Article 348Language to be used in Supreme Court and in High Courts and for Acts, Bills, etc.All proceedings in Supreme Court and High Courts, and authoritative texts of Acts and Bills, shall be in English language.Official Language
Article 350AFacilities for instruction in mother-tongue at primary stageEndeavour to provide adequate facilities for instruction in mother-tongue to children belonging to linguistic minority groups.Official Language
Article 351Directive for development of the Hindi languageDuty of the Union to promote the spread of Hindi so that it may serve as a medium of expression for all elements of the composite culture of India.Official Language
Article 352Proclamation of Emergency (National Emergency)President proclaims Emergency on grounds of war, external aggression, or armed rebellion, on written advice of Union Cabinet; approved by 2/3rd majority of Parliament.Emergency & Amendment
Article 353Effect of Proclamation of EmergencyExecutive power of Union extends to giving directions to States; Parliament empowered to make laws on State List.Emergency & Amendment
Article 355Duty of the Union to protect States against external aggression and internal disturbanceUnion duty to ensure government of every State is carried on in accordance with the Constitution.Emergency & Amendment
Article 356Provisions in case of failure of constitutional machinery in States (President’s Rule)President assumes executive powers of State and Parliament assumes legislative powers upon report of Governor; subject to judicial review (S.R. Bommai).Emergency & Amendment
Article 358Suspension of provisions of Article 19 during emergenciesArticle 19 freedoms automatically suspended only during emergency proclaimed on grounds of war or external aggression, not armed rebellion.Emergency & Amendment
Article 359Suspension of the enforcement of the rights conferred by Part III during emergenciesPresident order may suspend right to move court for fundamental rights, EXCEPT Articles 20 and 21 (which can never be suspended).Emergency & Amendment
Article 360Provisions as to Financial EmergencyPresident proclaims Financial Emergency if financial stability or credit of India threatened; salaries of civil servants and judges may be reduced.Emergency & Amendment
Article 361Protection of President and GovernorsImmunity from criminal proceedings and arrest during term of office; not answerable to any court for exercise of powers.Emergency & Amendment
Article 368Power of Parliament to amend the Constitution and procedure thereforParliament may amend by addition, variation or repeal; requires 2/3rd majority in both Houses; federal provisions require ratification by 50% of State Legislatures; basic structure cannot be altered (Kesavananda Bharati).Emergency & Amendment
Article 370Temporary provisions with respect to the State of Jammu and KashmirHistorically granted special status; rendered inoperative by Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Orders, 2019, upheld by Supreme Court in 2023.Temporary & Special Provisions
Article 371 to 371JSpecial provisions with respect to certain StatesSpecial provisions for Maharashtra, Gujarat, Nagaland, Assam, Manipur, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Sikkim, Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh, Goa, and Karnataka.Temporary & Special Provisions
Article 393-395Short title, Commencement, Authoritative Text in Hindi and RepealsConstitution of India came into force on 26th January, 1950; repealed Indian Independence Act, 1947 and Government of India Act, 1935.Temporary & Special Provisions
Schedules 1 to 12The Twelve Schedules to the Constitution of India1: States & UTs; 2: Emoluments; 3: Oaths; 4: Rajya Sabha seats; 5: Scheduled Areas; 6: Tribal Areas; 7: Union/State/Concurrent Lists; 8: 22 Official Languages; 9: Acts immune from judicial review; 10: Anti-defection law; 11: Panchayats (29 matters); 12: Municipalities (18 matters).Schedules
