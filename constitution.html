Constitution at a glance

A searchable quick reference to {len(ARTS)} key Articles of the Constitution of India, in plain language.

← Back to home

ArticleTitleIn plain languageGroup
Article 12Definition of “State”For Part III, “the State” includes the Government and Parliament, state governments and legislatures, and local and other authorities.Fundamental Rights
Article 13Laws inconsistent with Fundamental RightsLaws that conflict with Fundamental Rights are void to the extent of the inconsistency.Fundamental Rights
Article 14Equality before lawThe State shall not deny any person equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws.Fundamental Rights
Article 15Prohibition of discriminationNo discrimination on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth. Special provisions for women, children and backward classes are permitted.Fundamental Rights
Article 16Equality of opportunity in public employmentEqual opportunity in public jobs, with provision for reservation for backward classes not adequately represented.Fundamental Rights
Article 17Abolition of untouchabilityUntouchability is abolished and its practice in any form is forbidden.Fundamental Rights
Article 18Abolition of titlesThe State cannot confer titles, apart from military or academic distinctions.Fundamental Rights
Article 19Protection of certain freedomsCitizens have the freedoms of speech and expression, peaceful assembly, association, movement, residence and practising a profession or business, subject to reasonable restrictions.Fundamental Rights
Article 20Protection in respect of conviction for offencesNo retrospective criminal laws, no double jeopardy, and no compulsion to be a witness against oneself.Fundamental Rights
Article 21Protection of life and personal libertyNo person shall be deprived of life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law. Courts have read in fairness, privacy, livelihood and more.Fundamental Rights
Article 21ARight to educationFree and compulsory education for children aged six to fourteen.Fundamental Rights
Article 22Protection against arrest and detentionRights to be told the grounds of arrest, to consult a lawyer, and to be produced before a magistrate within 24 hours. Special rules apply to preventive detention.Fundamental Rights
Article 23Prohibition of trafficking and forced labourTraffic in human beings and forced labour are prohibited.Fundamental Rights
Article 24Prohibition of employment of childrenNo child below fourteen may be employed in a factory, mine or other hazardous work.Fundamental Rights
Article 25Freedom of religionFreedom of conscience and the right to profess, practise and propagate religion, subject to public order, morality and health.Fundamental Rights
Article 26Freedom to manage religious affairsReligious denominations may manage their own religious affairs and institutions, subject to public order, morality and health.Fundamental Rights
Article 27Freedom from taxes for religionNo one can be compelled to pay taxes for promoting a particular religion.Fundamental Rights
Article 28Freedom in religious instructionLimits on religious instruction in State-funded educational institutions.Fundamental Rights
Article 29Protection of minorities’ interestsAny section of citizens with a distinct language, script or culture has the right to conserve it.Fundamental Rights
Article 30Minorities’ right to educational institutionsMinorities have the right to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice.Fundamental Rights
Article 32Right to constitutional remediesThe right to move the Supreme Court to enforce Fundamental Rights, including through writs.Fundamental Rights
Article 36Definition of “State” in Part IVHas the same meaning as in Part III unless the context requires otherwise.Directive Principles and Duties
Article 37Application of Directive PrinciplesDirective Principles are not enforceable by courts, but are fundamental in governing the country.Directive Principles and Duties
Article 39AEqual justice and free legal aidThe State shall ensure that the legal system promotes justice on the basis of equal opportunity, including free legal aid.Directive Principles and Duties
Article 44Uniform civil codeThe State shall endeavour to secure a uniform civil code for citizens throughout India.Directive Principles and Duties
Article 48AProtection of the environmentThe State shall endeavour to protect and improve the environment and safeguard forests and wildlife.Directive Principles and Duties
Article 50Separation of judiciary from executiveThe State shall take steps to separate the judiciary from the executive in public services.Directive Principles and Duties
Article 51AFundamental DutiesA list of duties of every citizen, such as respecting the Constitution, protecting the environment and developing scientific temper.Directive Principles and Duties
Article 52The President of IndiaThere shall be a President of India.Union and Judiciary
Article 53Executive power of the UnionThe executive power of the Union is vested in the President.Union and Judiciary
Article 72Power of the President to grant pardonsThe President may grant pardons, reprieves, respites or remissions of punishment and suspend, remit or commute sentences in specified cases.Union and Judiciary
Article 74Council of Ministers to aid and advise PresidentThere shall be a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head to aid and advise the President.Union and Judiciary
Article 79Constitution of ParliamentParliament consists of the President and the two Houses: the Council of States and the House of the People.Union and Judiciary
Article 123Power of the President to promulgate ordinancesThe President can issue ordinances when Parliament is not in session, if circumstances require immediate action.Union and Judiciary
Article 124Establishment of the Supreme CourtProvides for the Supreme Court of India and its judges.Union and Judiciary
Article 129Supreme Court as a court of recordThe Supreme Court is a court of record with power to punish for contempt of itself.Union and Judiciary
Article 131Original jurisdiction of the Supreme CourtThe Supreme Court decides certain disputes between the Union and States, or between States, in its original jurisdiction.Union and Judiciary
Article 136Special leave to appealThe Supreme Court may grant special leave to appeal from almost any court or tribunal in India.Union and Judiciary
Article 141Law declared by the Supreme CourtThe law declared by the Supreme Court is binding on all courts in India.Union and Judiciary
Article 142Enforcement of decrees and ordersThe Supreme Court can pass such orders as are necessary to do complete justice in a case.Union and Judiciary
Article 143Power of the President to consult the Supreme CourtThe President may seek the Supreme Court’s advisory opinion on a question of law or fact.Union and Judiciary
Article 161Power of the Governor to grant pardonsThe Governor may grant pardons and similar relief in matters within the State’s executive power.Union and Judiciary
Article 213Power of the Governor to promulgate ordinancesThe Governor can issue ordinances when the State Legislature is not in session.Union and Judiciary
Article 214High Courts for StatesThere shall be a High Court for each State.Union and Judiciary
Article 215High Courts as courts of recordHigh Courts are courts of record and can punish for contempt of themselves.Union and Judiciary
Article 226Power of High Courts to issue writsHigh Courts can issue writs to enforce Fundamental Rights and for any other purpose.Union and Judiciary
Article 227Superintendence over courtsEvery High Court has superintendence over all courts and tribunals in its territory.Union and Judiciary
Article 245Extent of laws made by Parliament and StatesParliament and State Legislatures make laws for their territories, subject to the Constitution.Other
Article 246Subject-matter of lawsDivides legislative subjects among the Union, State and Concurrent Lists in the Seventh Schedule.Other
Article 300APersons not to be deprived of property save by lawNo person shall be deprived of property except by authority of law. This is a constitutional right, not a Fundamental Right.Other
Article 324Election CommissionSuperintendence, direction and control of elections is vested in the Election Commission.Other
Article 326Adult suffrageElections to the House of the People and State Assemblies are on the basis of adult suffrage.Other
Article 352Proclamation of EmergencyThe President can proclaim an Emergency on grounds such as war, external aggression or armed rebellion, subject to conditions.Emergency and Amendment
Article 356Failure of constitutional machinery in StatesProvides for President’s Rule in a State. The power is subject to judicial review (S.R. Bommai).Emergency and Amendment
Article 360Financial EmergencyProvides for a Financial Emergency if the financial stability or credit of India is threatened.Emergency and Amendment
Article 368Power of Parliament to amend the ConstitutionParliament may amend the Constitution, but not its basic structure (Kesavananda Bharati).Emergency and Amendment
