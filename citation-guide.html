Legal citation guide

How to cite cases, statutes, books, articles and websites in OSCOLA and the Indian Bluebook format.

← Back to home

A citation lets your reader find and check your source. Choose one style, follow it from the first footnote to the last, and apply it consistently. Mixing styles is one of the most common marks of weak legal writing.

Which style? Use the style your journal, competition or university asks for. Many Indian law schools and journals use OSCOLA or an Indian adaptation of the Bluebook. This guide covers both. The citation generator formats case and statute citations for you.

At a glance

OSCOLABluebook (Indian law school adaptation)
Case nameItalics, “v” without a full stopItalics, “v.” with a full stop
StatuteAct Year, s numberAct, year, § number, No., Acts of Parliament, year (India)
Article titleSingle quotation marks, not italicItalic or plain, as your guide says
Book authorAuthor name in normal caseAuthor and title in small capitals
Repeat citationibid, or Author (n 3)Id., or Author, supra note 3

OSCOLA examples

SourceFormat and example
CaseManeka Gandhi v Union of India (1978) 1 SCC 248
StatuteIndian Contract Act 1872, s 10
ConstitutionConstitution of India 1950, art 21
BookAuthor Name, Title of Book (4th edn, Publisher 2020) 45
Journal articleAuthor Name, ‘Title of the Article’ (2021) 12 Journal Name 55, 60
WebsiteOrganisation or Author, ‘Title of the Page’ (Website Name, date) <URL> accessed 5 March 2026
Shortened formibid (same source as the previous footnote), or Maneka Gandhi (n 4) for an earlier source

OSCOLA does not use full stops after “s”, “ss” or “art”. Case names are italicised in the original, and the year goes in square brackets when it is part of the report’s name and in round brackets when it is not. Add a pinpoint page or paragraph after the first page.

Bluebook (Indian law school adaptation) examples

SourceFormat and example
CaseManeka Gandhi v. Union of India, (1978) 1 SCC 248.
With pinpoint: Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India, (1978) 1 SCC 248, 281.
StatuteRight to Information Act, 2005, § 6, No. 22, Acts of Parliament, 2005 (India).
ConstitutionINDIA CONST. art. 21.
BookAUTHOR NAME, TITLE OF BOOK 45 (4th ed. Publisher 2020).
Journal articleAuthor Name, Title of the Article, 12 J. NAME 55, 60 (2021).
WebsiteNAME OF WEBSITE, https://example.org/page (last visited Mar. 5, 2026).
Shortened formId. (same source as the previous footnote), Id. at 70 (different page), or AUTHOR, supra note 10, at 89.

For Acts passed before 1950, the “Acts of Parliament” phrase may not fit. Check what your style guide says about the enacting body.

Citing Indian cases

  • Give the case name, then the report citation, such as SCC, AIR or SCR.
  • The same case is often reported in several places. Use the report your guide prefers, and be consistent.
  • Cases without a printed report citation can be cited by court, case number and year, or with a neutral citation if one exists.
  • Always give the court if it is not clear from the citation.

Rules that apply to every style

  • Cite every legal proposition and every fact you take from a source.
  • Put footnotes at the bottom of the page where the source appears, numbered continuously from 1, in a smaller font than the body text.
  • Prefer primary sources: Acts, judgments and treaties. Use secondary sources to support them.
  • Verify every case name, section number, author and page against the original.
  • Do not cite Wikipedia, unverified blogs or AI-generated text as authority.
  • Do not put two unrelated sources in one footnote.

Common errors

  • Mixing OSCOLA and Bluebook features in one article.
  • Missing pinpoints.
  • Citing a section of the old IPC, CrPC or Evidence Act when the new code applies. Check the section finder.
  • Putting the year in the wrong kind of brackets.
  • Giving a website without an access date.
