Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023

A chapter-by-chapter student guide to the BNS, with key sections explained in plain language.

← Back to home
About this guide. This guide follows the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (Act No. 45 of 2023) as published in the Gazette of India. It replaces the Indian Penal Code for offences committed on or after 1 July 2024. Section headings are taken from the official text, and the explanations are our own summaries. They leave out sub-sections, provisos and exceptions. Read the section itself on India Code before you cite it. Last updated: October 2026.

Chapter III: General ExceptionsSections 14 to 44＋

Situations where an act is not an offence, for example acts of a very young child, a person of unsound mind or someone acting by accident or mistake, and the right of private defence of body and property.

Key sections explained

  • BNS 20. Act of a child under seven years of age.
    A child under seven years cannot commit an offence. Section 21 covers a child aged seven to twelve who has not reached enough maturity to understand the nature and consequences of the conduct.
  • BNS 22. Act of a person of unsound mind.
    A person who, because of unsoundness of mind, cannot understand the nature of the act or that it is wrong or against the law, does not commit an offence.
  • BNS 35. Right of private defence of body and of property.
    The right of private defence of the body and of property, subject to the limits in Section 37. Section 38 explains when this right can extend to causing death.

Chapter V: Of Offences Against Woman and ChildSections 63 to 99＋

Sexual offences and offences against women and children, including rape, gang rape, sexual intercourse by deceitful means, sexual harassment, stalking, dowry death, cruelty by a husband or his relatives, and offences such as selling a child.

Key sections explained

  • BNS 63. Rape.
    Defines rape and the acts that amount to it.Earlier law: IPC 375
  • BNS 64. Punishment for rape.Earlier law: IPC 376
  • BNS 69. Sexual intercourse by employing deceitful means, etc.
    Makes it an offence to have sexual intercourse by deceitful means or with a false promise to marry, where it is not rape. “Deceitful means” includes a false promise of a job or promotion or marrying by hiding identity. This is a new offence.Earlier law: No IPC equivalent
  • BNS 74. Assault or use of criminal force to woman with intent to outrage her modesty.
    Assault or criminal force against a woman intending to outrage her modesty. The minimum term is one year.Earlier law: IPC 354
  • BNS 75. Sexual harassment.
    Sexual harassment, including unwelcome physical contact, demands for sexual favours and showing pornography against a woman’s will.Earlier law: IPC 354A
  • BNS 78. Stalking.
    Stalking: repeatedly following a woman or contacting her despite clear disinterest, or monitoring her use of the internet or email. There are exceptions, for example for crime prevention.Earlier law: IPC 354D
  • BNS 80. Dowry death.
    Dowry death: the death of a woman within seven years of marriage, not under normal circumstances, where she was subjected to cruelty or harassment for dowry soon before her death.Earlier law: IPC 304B
  • BNS 85. Husband or relative of husband of a woman subjecting her to cruelty.
    Cruelty by the husband or his relative towards a woman.Earlier law: IPC 498A

Chapter VI: Of Offences Affecting the Human BodySections 100 to 146＋

Offences that affect the human body: culpable homicide and murder, abetment of suicide, attempt to murder, organised crime and terrorist acts, hurt and grievous hurt, wrongful restraint and confinement, criminal force and assault, kidnapping and unlawful compulsory labour.

Key sections explained

  • BNS 100. Culpable homicide.
    Defines culpable homicide: causing death with the intention to cause death or bodily injury likely to cause death, or with knowledge that death is likely.Earlier law: IPC 299
  • BNS 101. Murder.
    Defines murder, which is culpable homicide that falls within the listed circumstances, subject to the exceptions.Earlier law: IPC 300
  • BNS 103. Punishment for murder.
    Sub-section (2) adds a provision for murder by a group of five or more persons on certain grounds.Earlier law: IPC 302
  • BNS 105. Punishment for culpable homicide not amounting to murder.Earlier law: IPC 304
  • BNS 106. Causing death by negligence.Earlier law: IPC 304A
  • BNS 108. Abetment of suicide.Earlier law: IPC 306
  • BNS 109. Attempt to murder.Earlier law: IPC 307
  • BNS 111. Organised crime.
    Defines and punishes organised crime: continuing unlawful activity by a group or syndicate, using violence, threats or coercion to gain a benefit. This is a new offence.Earlier law: No IPC equivalent
  • BNS 113. Terrorist act.
    Defines and punishes a terrorist act: an act intended to threaten the unity, integrity, sovereignty or security of India or to strike terror. This is a new offence in the BNS.Earlier law: No IPC equivalent
  • BNS 115. Voluntarily causing hurt.
    Sub-section (2) is the basic punishment.Earlier law: IPC 323
  • BNS 126. Wrongful restraint.
    Wrongful restraint: voluntarily obstructing a person from going where they have a right to go.Earlier law: IPC 339
  • BNS 137. Kidnapping.
    Kidnapping, of two kinds: from India and from lawful guardianship.Earlier law: IPC 359 to 361

Offences against property: theft, snatching, extortion, robbery, dacoity, criminal misappropriation, criminal breach of trust, cheating, mischief and criminal trespass.

Key sections explained

  • BNS 303. Theft.Earlier law: IPC 378
  • BNS 304. Snatching.
    Snatching: theft by suddenly or forcibly seizing or taking property from a person. It is a separate offence in the BNS.Earlier law: No IPC equivalent
  • BNS 308. Extortion.
    Extortion: intentionally putting a person in fear of injury and dishonestly making them hand over property.Earlier law: IPC 383
  • BNS 309. Robbery.
    Robbery: theft or extortion accompanied by death, hurt, wrongful restraint or fear of these.Earlier law: IPC 390
  • BNS 310. Dacoity.
    Dacoity: robbery committed or attempted by five or more persons together.Earlier law: IPC 391
  • BNS 316. Criminal breach of trust.
    Criminal breach of trust: dishonestly misappropriating or using property that has been entrusted to you.Earlier law: IPC 405
  • BNS 318. Cheating.
    Cheating: deceiving a person to dishonestly induce them to hand over property or to do or not do something. Section 318(4) covers cheating that dishonestly induces delivery of property.Earlier law: IPC 415, 420
  • BNS 324. Mischief. f h
