Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023

A chapter-by-chapter student guide to the BSA, with key sections explained in plain language.

← Back to home
About this guide. This guide follows the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (Act No. 47 of 2023) in the India Code version updated to 6 October 2025. It replaces the Indian Evidence Act, 1872. Section headings are taken from the official text, and the explanations are our own summaries. They leave out sub-sections, provisos and exceptions. Read the section itself on India Code before you cite it. Last updated: October 2026.

Chapter II: Relevancy of FactsSections 3 to 50＋

Which facts can be proved in court: facts in issue and relevant facts, confessions and admissions, statements of people who cannot be called, expert opinion, character and the standing of judgments.

Key sections explained

  • BSA 3. Evidence may be given of facts in issue and relevant facts.
    Evidence may be given of facts in issue and relevant facts, and of no others.Earlier law: Evidence Act 5
  • BSA 22. Confession caused by inducement, threat, coercion or promise, when irrelevant in criminal proceeding.
    A confession caused by inducement, threat, coercion or promise is not relevant in a criminal proceeding.Earlier law: Evidence Act 24
  • BSA 23. Confession to police officer.
    No confession made to a police officer can be proved against the accused. A confession made in police custody cannot be proved unless made in the immediate presence of a Magistrate. Information that leads to the discovery of a fact can be proved to the extent it relates distinctly to that fact.Earlier law: Evidence Act 25, 26, 27
  • BSA 26. Cases in which statement of relevant fact by person who is dead or cannot be found, etc., is relevant.
    Statements of persons who are dead or cannot be found are relevant in the listed cases, including statements about the cause of their death.Earlier law: Evidence Act 32
  • BSA 39. Opinions of experts.
    Opinions of experts are relevant on specified subjects.Earlier law: Evidence Act 45

Chapter V: Documentary EvidenceSections 56 to 93＋

Documentary evidence: primary and secondary evidence, electronic and digital records, proof of signatures and attested documents, and public documents.

Key sections explained

  • BSA 57. Primary evidence.Earlier law: Evidence Act 62
  • BSA 58. Secondary evidence.Earlier law: Evidence Act 63
  • BSA 61. Electronic or digital record.
    An electronic or digital record cannot be denied admissibility only because it is electronic. Subject to Section 63, it has the same legal effect as other documents.
  • BSA 62. Special provisions as to evidence relating to electronic record.
    The contents of electronic records can be proved under Section 63.
  • BSA 63. Admissibility of electronic records.
    Computer output is treated as a document and is admissible without producing the original if the conditions in the section are met. A certificate, for which a format is given in the Schedule, has to be submitted with the electronic record.Earlier law: Evidence Act 65B

Chapter VII: The Burden of ProofSections 104 to 120＋

Burden of proof and presumptions, including presumptions about documents, electronic records, dowry death and consent in rape cases.

Key sections explained

  • BSA 106. Burden of proof as to particular fact.
    Burden of proof as to a particular fact.Earlier law: Evidence Act 106
  • BSA 118. Presumption as to dowry death.
    Presumption as to dowry death: where a woman was subjected to cruelty or harassment for dowry soon before her death, the court presumes the person caused the dowry death.
  • BSA 120. Presumption as to absence of consent in certain prosecution for rape.
    In a rape prosecution under Section 64(2) of the BNS, if sexual intercourse is proved and the woman says in court that she did not consent, the court presumes she did not consent.
